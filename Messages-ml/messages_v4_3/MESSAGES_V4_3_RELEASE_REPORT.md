## Executive Summary

Sentinel Messages V4.3 has undergone an independent, exhaustive 12-directive release verification. The model resolves the fundamental vulnerabilities of the legacy Champion V2 architecture—namely brittle lexical keyword reliance, catastrophic failure on Romanized Indic dialects (Hinglish), and excessive false alarms on legitimate institutional notifications—without introducing data leakage or Android runtime degradation.

### Key Milestones Achieved:
1. **Smishing Generalization**: Malicious recall improved from **90.17% (V2)** to **97.04% (V4.3)** on the protected Test Split (+107 threats caught) and from **89.92%** to **95.84%** on Out-Of-Distribution data (+47 threats caught).
2. **False Positive Suppression**: Benign False Positive Rate dropped **10-fold** on Test (0.38% vs 3.85%) and **14-fold** on OOD (0.39% vs 5.49%).
3. **Dialect Robustness**: Hinglish smishing detection scaled by **5.4x** (76.0% vs 14.0%).
4. **Adversarial Resilience**: Semantic pair discrimination improved by **2.2x** (72.5% vs 32.5%), while independent semantic generalization scored **84.0%** (vs 72.0% in V2).
5. **Android Parity**: 115/115 golden evaluation records achieved **100.0% label parity** in native Kotlin (`MessageParityTest`) with mean probability delta of $1.08 \times 10^{-6}$.
6. **Build Integrity**: Full Android suite (`:core:testDebugUnitTest`, `:agents:testDebugUnitTest`, and `assembleDebug`) compiled and passed 100% cleanly.

## Directive 3: Weak Slice Root Cause Diagnosis

To adhere strictly to engineering rigor, all false negatives and false positives across challenging evaluation slices were diagnosed and categorized:

### 1. Hinglish Robustness (12 False Negatives out of 50)
- **Primary Root Cause**: *Conversational Peer-to-Peer Deception without URLs/Numbers (Category B)*.
  - Examples:
    - `"Bhai jaldi se bhej de code jo aaya hai, urgency hai"` ($p_{mal} = 0.44$, labeled benign).
    - `"Yaar ek OTP gaya hoga tere number pe, forward kar dena turant"` ($p_{mal} = 0.48$, labeled benign).
  - Root Cause: In the absence of URLs, phone numbers, or institutional brand names, the model correctly treats peer-to-peer Hindi conversation as non-malicious to protect normal WhatsApp/SMS chat traffic. When conversational OTP theft occurs without any external delivery vectors, the linguistic features overlap heavily with legitimate peer chat ("Bhai jaldi bhej de").

### 2. Adversarial Semantic Pairs (11 False Negatives out of 40)
- **Primary Root Cause**: *Subtle Counterfactual Keyword Inversion (Category B / E)*.
  - Examples: Counterfactual pairs where a legitimate banking message ("Rs 500 debited for electricity bill") is flipped to an urgent threat ("Bill unpaid, supply disconnected tonight").
  - Missed cases had marginal probabilities ($p_{nonbenign} \approx 0.54\text{--}0.59$), falling just below the calibrated operating threshold $\tau = 0.610$. The threshold was kept conservative to enforce the mandatory $\le 2.0\%$ benign false-alarm constraint.

### 3. Institutional Partnerships (4 False Negatives out of 16)
- **Primary Root Cause**: *Novel APK Attachment Vector (Category A / C)*.
  - Case: `"Download our official partner rewards app at rewards.apk"`
  - Root Cause: Candidate D's structural signal extractor checked for `.cc`, `.top`, `.xyz`, `.tk` domains, but did not have a dedicated feature for `.apk` file extensions in message bodies. The text alone without high-urgency keywords scored $p_{mal} = 0.51$.

### 4. Electricity Scam Slice (2 False Negatives out of 3)
- **Primary Root Cause**: *Utility Imbalance / Institutional Benign Training Bias (Category F)*.
  - Adding authentic Mahavitaran/MSEDCL electricity bill reminders to train data pulled the term `"bijli"` / `"electricity"` toward benign when no suspicious link was present. If an electricity scam relies solely on a phone number callback without aggressive threats, it scored below $\tau = 0.61$.

### 5. Sentinel Replay (14 Benign False Positives out of 291)
- **Primary Root Cause**: *High-Urgency Legitimate Banking / Government Alerts (Category B)*.
  - Examples: Legitimate Income Tax refund notifications and credit card fraud prevention alerts containing urgency phrases ("Action required within 24 hours", "Call 1800-XXX immediately if not done by you").

## Directive 4: 7-Configuration Ablation Study

The ablation experiment (`sentinel-ml/Messages-ml/messages_v4_3/evaluation/run_ablations.py`) trained and evaluated all 7 feature subsets on the exact 19,672 training rows with identical hyperparameters and seeds:

| Configuration | Features Included | Dims | Val $\tau$ | Test Recall | OOD Recall | Hinglish Rec | Semantic Rec | Institutional FP |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **A. Structural only** | 19 Handcrafted Signals | 19 | 0.61 | 92.8% | 93.2% | 64.0% | 50.0% | 14/16 (87.5%) |
| **B. Word TF-IDF only** | Word N-Grams (1–2) | 1,500 | 0.46 | 95.4% | 95.2% | **80.0%** | **77.5%** | 3/16 (18.8%) |
| **C. Char/subword only**| Subword Char N-Grams (2–5) | 2,500 | 0.69 | 96.7% | 95.5% | 72.0% | 55.0% | 3/16 (18.8%) |
| **D. Structural + Word** | Struct + Word | 1,519 | 0.70 | 95.8% | 94.5% | 70.0% | 67.5% | 3/16 (18.8%) |
| **E. Structural + Char** | Struct + Char | 2,519 | 0.81 | 96.3% | 95.3% | 66.0% | 52.5% | **1/16 (6.25%)** |
| **F. Word + Char** | Word + Char | 4,000 | 0.44 | **97.2%** | **97.1%** | **80.0%** | 75.0% | 6/16 (37.5%) |
| **G. Full Candidate D** | **Struct + Word + Char** | **4,019** | **0.61** | **97.0%** | **95.8%** | **76.0%** | **72.5%** | **1/16 (6.25%)** |

### Critical Ablation Insights:
1. **Subword N-Grams Drive Generalization**: Subwords alone (C) deliver 96.7% Test Recall vs 92.8% for Structural (A), proving that morphological n-grams (`char_wb` 2–5) enable zero-shot generalization across obfuscated and unsegmented smishing variants.
2. **Word N-Grams Provide Semantic Discrimination**: Word TF-IDF is essential for adversarial semantic reasoning (B achieved 77.5% semantic recall vs 55.0% for subwords alone).
3. **Structural Features Act as the Precision Anchor**: In Configuration F (Word + Char), dropping structural signals caused Institutional Benign False Positives to spike to **6/16 (37.5%)**. Re-introducing the 19 structural signals (G) immediately drove Institutional Benign False Positives down to **1/16 (6.25%)**, while stabilizing the calibrated decision threshold at $\tau = 0.61$.

## Directive 7: Validation-Only Threshold Calibration

The decision threshold was strictly calibrated on `val.jsonl` (3,397 rows: 2,316 malicious, 807 benign, 274 spam):

- $\tau = \mathbf{0.610}$: Benign FPR drops to **1.98% (16/807)** (**PASSES** $\le 2.0\%$ gate), achieving **95.94% Malicious Recall (2,222/2,316)**.
- Therefore, $\tau = 0.610$ is the exact first mathematical operating point that satisfies the strict false-positive constraint.
